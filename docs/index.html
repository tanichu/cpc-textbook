0.1 What this book is

Collective Predictive Coding (CPC) is presented here as a mathematical theory of symbol emergence systems. The book has three aims:

  1. To give CPC a systematic mathematical formulation as a theory of symbol emergence systems.
  2. To unify formulations that are currently scattered across individual papers, deriving them as successive elaborations of a single generative model.
  3. To be usable as a foundational text beyond robotics — for CPC-MS and, more broadly, for theories of symbol systems and intelligence.

0.2 Positioning

CPC is not a theory of symbol emergence. It is a theory of symbol emergence systems. Its object is not only the process by which symbols arise, but the system in which the cognitive dynamics of individuals and the dynamics of a society’s symbol system organize each other.

Three terms are kept distinct throughout the book:

Term What it refers to
Symbol system The product of emergence. Corresponds to w.
Symbol emergence The process by which it arises.
Symbol emergence system The system in which that process takes place.

The Springer book Symbol Emergence Systems discusses what such systems are and how they look as a whole; this book supplies their mathematics. The two are companion volumes.

This book is not a general survey of symbol emergence in robotics. Not repeating the structure of Symbol Emergence in Robotics is an explicit constraint on its design.

0.2.1 Intended readers

  • Primary: graduate students and researchers in informatics, cognitive science, and robotics.
  • Secondary: graduate students in linguistics, anthropology, sociology, science studies, and economics.

Variational Bayes is assumed as the lower bound of mathematical background; mathematical preliminaries are collected in the appendices rather than in the main text.

0.3 The core formulation

The whole book is organized around one generative model,

p(w) \prod_{k=1}^{K} p(z_k \mid w)\, p(x_k \mid z_k),

and the collective posterior it induces,

P(w \mid x_{1:K}) \;\propto\; p(w) \prod_{k=1}^{K} \int p(x_k \mid z_k)\, p(z_k \mid w)\, \mathrm{d}z_k .

Two constraints give this model its meaning:

Operational closure. Agent k has no access whatsoever to z_j for j \neq k.

Arbitrariness. The relation between w and z is not an invertible map but a general probabilistic relation P(w \mid z). Dimensions and types need not agree, and w may be discrete or continuous.

Chapters 1 and 2 state this formulation. Every later chapter can be read as a commentary on Chapter 2 — extensions are presented as differences from it: put w_{1:T} into the equation, multiply in an optimality-variable term, let w be continuous.

Each chapter is built around a single proposition, typically of the form: language game G is a correct inference algorithm for the posterior of generative model \mathcal{M}. The theory is the collection of these propositions.

0.4 How to read it

Readers who do not want to follow the mathematics can read

Chapters 1–2 → Chapter 13 → Chapter 24 → Part VI → Chapter 31

and skip the rest. This is a legitimate path through the book, not a shortcut. Every chapter opens with an intuitive summary in one paragraph and no equations, Appendix D gives a correspondence table between humanities/social-science and mathematical vocabulary, and Part VI does not depend on Part II.
